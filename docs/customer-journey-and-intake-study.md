# Burooj and iinvestinsaudi Intake Study

Date: 2026-05-09

## Objective

Build a shared inquiry and review experience across:

- `buroojconsulting.com`: the parent advisory firm for management consulting, legal advisory coordination, feasibility studies, SIDF preparation, soft-landing, and partner-law-firm representation.
- `iinvestinsaudi.com`: the investor-facing opportunity and Saudi market-entry platform.

The desired experience is that a client or investor can choose the right pathway, submit structured information, upload attachments, receive confirmation, and have the request reviewed internally. After review, the request should be marked as accepted, pending more information, or rejected, with clear communication back to the client.

## Strategic Position

Burooj should not feel like a generic contact form. It should feel like a professional advisory intake desk for serious Saudi market decisions.

The client should understand:

- Which service path fits their need.
- What information Burooj needs to assess the request.
- What happens after submission.
- How legal representation is coordinated through the partner law firm.
- That uploaded material is treated as confidential review material.

iinvestinsaudi should act as the discovery and opportunity layer. Burooj should act as the advisory, documentation, legal coordination, and execution layer.

## Competitor Benchmarks

### Invest Saudi

Source: https://eservices.sagia.gov.sa/investsaudistorefront/investsaudi/en/login

Strengths:

- Uses registration before opportunity submission.
- Sends registration confirmation email.
- Has a logged-in environment for opportunities, events, resources, and business support.
- Separates opportunity participation from business support.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- Government-platform tone can feel formal and broad.
- The journey is not optimized for boutique advisory conversion.
- It does not combine feasibility, legal coordination, and practical soft-landing into one guided request experience.

What to learn:

- Use confirmation emails and a request/account concept.
- Make the status journey visible and credible.
- Keep the first step lighter than full registration, then deepen the data request based on the selected pathway.

### SIDF Contact Flow

Source: https://www.sidf.gov.sa/en/Pages/contactUs.aspx

Strengths:

- Uses service category and purpose of request.
- Includes subject, message, contact details, privacy acceptance, and official support channels.
- Clearly separates inquiry support from technical support.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- It is a support/contact form, not a guided feasibility/SIDF advisory intake.
- No visible prequalification logic.
- No document checklist at the inquiry stage.

What to learn:

- Add service category and purpose.
- Include explicit privacy/consent language.
- Use structured fields so requests can be triaged quickly.

### Qirat Advisory

Source: https://qirat-advisory.com/soft-landing/

Strengths:

- Strong soft-landing narrative.
- Explains what the client gets.
- Includes phased methodology and deliverables.
- Uses meeting scheduling and company profile download.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- No visible request tracker or document intake.
- The client still has to infer which materials to prepare.
- No clear status lifecycle after contact.

What to learn:

- Explain deliverables before the form.
- Make the pathway feel operational, not only advisory.
- Offer direct meeting scheduling after a structured request is submitted.

### AdProcure Soft Landing

Source: https://adprocure.com/soft-landing-management-service

Strengths:

- Clearly defines soft landing as end-to-end setup: formation, office, banking, talent, suppliers, and PRO services.
- Simple language focused on practical execution.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- Basic lead capture.
- No investor-grade feasibility or SIDF pathway.
- No status or review workflow.

What to learn:

- Soft-landing pages should describe practical work, not just consulting.
- Use plain deliverable lists.

### Saudi Investment Gate

Source: https://www.gatetosaudi.com/

Strengths:

- Strong market-entry positioning.
- Uses service categories similar to the Burooj target: company formation, market intelligence, feasibility, strategic alliances, and soft-landing.
- Frames the firm as a strategic partner for global corporations.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- The funnel appears consultation-led rather than structured request-led.
- Less emphasis on request status, document review, and client case progression.

What to learn:

- Keep the strategic tone high.
- Pair that tone with a more concrete intake and review system.

### Saudi Law Firm Foreign-Investor Pages

Examples:

- https://attorney.sa/
- https://adelqaff.com/en/international-investors.html
- https://mashoralaw.com/foreign-investment/

Strengths:

- Strong legal authority and foreign-investor wording.
- Clear MISA, company formation, licensing, compliance, and representation language.
- Some use consultation forms with service type and notes.

Weaknesses/opportunity for Burooj:

- Most legal forms are generic.
- They rarely integrate management advisory, feasibility, SIDF documentation, and opportunity discovery.
- They do not usually provide a transparent post-submission review path.

What to learn:

- Legal representation requests need conflict/scope screening.
- Representation must be clearly separated from Burooj advisory unless handled by the partner law firm.

## Recommended Customer Journey

### Entry Points

1. Burooj homepage:
   - Start advisory request.
   - Choose consulting, legal advisory, legal representation, feasibility/SIDF, soft-landing, or general Saudi market entry.

2. iinvestinsaudi opportunity card:
   - Request this opportunity.
   - Ask for feasibility/SIDF support.
   - Ask for soft-landing support.
   - Ask to speak about Saudi market entry.

3. Direct contact:
   - Email, WhatsApp, and phone should remain available, but the primary route should be the structured request.

### User Segments

1. International investor:
   - Wants to understand whether Saudi entry is practical.
   - Needs licensing path, entity setup, banking/admin readiness, and local introductions.

2. Saudi company:
   - Wants feasibility, financing documentation, SIDF preparation, expansion, or partnerships.

3. Opportunity investor:
   - Finds a project on iinvestinsaudi.
   - Needs more details, feasibility review, market validation, or introductions.

4. Legal client:
   - Needs legal advisory coordination or formal legal representation through Muhammad Gamal Rushdi Law Firm.

5. Lender/stakeholder:
   - Needs structured documentation, assumptions, feasibility support, or review-ready materials.

## Ideal Request Flow

### Step 1: Choose Request Pathway

Options:

- Management consulting
- Legal consulting coordination
- Legal representation through partner law firm
- Feasibility study
- SIDF submission preparation
- Foreign investor soft-landing
- Specific opportunity from iinvestinsaudi
- General Saudi market-entry question

### Step 2: Applicant Details

Required:

- Full name
- Work email
- Phone/WhatsApp
- Company or organization
- Country
- Applicant type: Saudi company, foreign company, individual investor, lender/stakeholder, legal client, other

Recommended:

- Website/LinkedIn
- Preferred language
- Preferred contact method

### Step 3: Request Details

Shared:

- Request title
- Selected service pathway
- Short description
- Target sector
- Saudi location, if known
- Timeline
- Estimated budget or investment size
- Urgency

Pathway-specific:

- Legal representation: matter type, counterparty, jurisdiction, court/authority if any, conflict-check fields, partner-law-firm consent.
- Feasibility/SIDF: project type, capex range, current data availability, existing model/study status, land/site status, financing target.
- Soft landing: current incorporation status, MISA license status, entity type, number of employees planned, office/banking needs.
- Opportunity inquiry: opportunity ID, opportunity name, intended role, investment appetite, required information.

### Step 4: Attachments

Accepted files:

- PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, CSV, JPG, PNG

Suggested uploads:

- Company profile
- Existing feasibility study
- Financial model
- Commercial registration or license
- Project brief
- Land/site information
- Legal documents
- SIDF-related materials

Limits:

- 10 files maximum in first version.
- 25 MB per file.
- 100 MB total per request.

Security:

- Store files in a private Supabase Storage bucket.
- Never email attachment files directly if they contain sensitive data; email secure internal links instead.
- Use signed URLs for admin review.

### Step 5: Confirmation

Client sees:

- Request reference number, e.g. `BRJ-2026-000127`.
- Current status: `Under review`.
- Expected review timing: e.g. "We usually complete the initial review within 2-3 business days."
- Next steps.

Client receives:

- Confirmation email.
- Summary of request details.
- Reference number.
- Note that Burooj may request further information.

### Step 6: Internal Review

Internal review checklist:

- Service fit.
- Sector fit.
- Document completeness.
- Commercial priority.
- Legal conflict/scope check where representation is requested.
- SIDF readiness level where financing support is requested.
- Soft-landing readiness level where foreign entry is requested.

### Step 7: Status Decision

Statuses:

- `submitted`: request received.
- `under_review`: Burooj is screening the request.
- `pending_more_information`: client must provide more material.
- `accepted`: Burooj is ready to proceed to consultation/scoping/engagement.
- `rejected`: Burooj cannot assist.
- `closed`: completed, withdrawn, or inactive.

Client-facing labels:

- Submitted
- Under review
- More information requested
- Accepted for next step
- Not a fit at this stage
- Closed

### Step 8: Status Email Templates

Accepted:

- Thank the client.
- Confirm Burooj reviewed the request.
- Provide next action: consultation booking, engagement proposal, or document request.
- Include confidentiality and legal representation note if applicable.

Pending more information:

- Explain that review cannot continue without specific items.
- Provide upload link or reply path.
- List missing documents clearly.

Rejected:

- Polite, professional.
- Avoid legal conclusions.
- Mention that the request is not a fit at this stage.
- Suggest contacting another relevant authority/professional only when appropriate.

## Backend Architecture

Recommended stack:

- Supabase Postgres for request records and status history.
- Supabase Storage for private attachments.
- Supabase Edge Functions for request creation, validation, file-link generation, and email notifications.
- Resend or Hostinger SMTP for email delivery.
- Admin-only dashboard protected by Supabase Auth.

Supabase docs:

- Edge Functions: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions
- Sending emails from Edge Functions: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/send-emails
- Storage: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage
- Storage access control: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/security/access-control

## Proposed Database Model

### `requests`

Core request table.

Fields:

- `id uuid primary key`
- `reference text unique not null`
- `source_site text not null` values: `burooj`, `iinvestinsaudi`
- `source_path text`
- `opportunity_id text`
- `service_path text not null`
- `status text not null default 'submitted'`
- `priority text default 'normal'`
- `applicant_type text`
- `full_name text not null`
- `email text not null`
- `phone text`
- `company text`
- `country text`
- `preferred_language text`
- `preferred_contact_method text`
- `title text`
- `message text not null`
- `sector text`
- `target_location text`
- `investment_size text`
- `timeline text`
- `metadata jsonb default '{}'`
- `created_at timestamptz default now()`
- `updated_at timestamptz default now()`

### `request_documents`

Fields:

- `id uuid primary key`
- `request_id uuid references requests(id)`
- `bucket text not null`
- `path text not null`
- `file_name text not null`
- `mime_type text`
- `file_size bigint`
- `uploaded_by text default 'client'`
- `created_at timestamptz default now()`

### `request_events`

Fields:

- `id uuid primary key`
- `request_id uuid references requests(id)`
- `event_type text not null`
- `from_status text`
- `to_status text`
- `note text`
- `created_by uuid`
- `created_at timestamptz default now()`

### `request_messages`

Fields:

- `id uuid primary key`
- `request_id uuid references requests(id)`
- `direction text not null` values: `internal`, `outbound`, `inbound`
- `subject text`
- `body text`
- `created_by uuid`
- `created_at timestamptz default now()`

## Differentiation Strategy

Burooj can beat most competitors by combining:

- Strategic Saudi market-entry positioning.
- Real structured intake.
- Secure document upload.
- Request reference number.
- Transparent review states.
- Clear separation between advisory and licensed legal representation.
- Opportunity-led journey through iinvestinsaudi.
- Feasibility/SIDF-specific intake fields.
- Soft-landing readiness pathway.

This makes the experience feel like an institutional advisory desk instead of a simple agency contact page.
